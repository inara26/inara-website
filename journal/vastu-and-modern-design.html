Vastu and Contemporary Design Can Live Together

By Ar. Bharath Munusamy4 August 20265 min read

A question we hear in almost every first meeting: can we have a contemporary home that also follows Vastu? The short answer is yes. The longer answer is that the best results come when Vastu is part of the conversation from the first sketch, not a correction applied to a finished plan.

Where Vastu and good design agree

Many traditional guidelines line up with sound climatic design. A kitchen that receives morning light from the east stays fresher. Heavier, more enclosed spaces on the south and west shield the home from the hottest sun. An open, uncluttered centre lets air and light move through the plan. These are principles any architect working in Tamil Nadu would recognise.

Plan first, then elevate

We fix the position of the entrance, kitchen, pooja room, master bedroom and staircase early, because they shape everything else. Once the plan is settled, the elevation is free to be as modern as you like. Vastu speaks to the arrangement of spaces, not to whether your facade has timber fins or a curved balcony.

Tell us early what matters to you. A plan built around your priorities is always better than one bent to fit them later.

When the plot does not cooperate

Urban plots are rarely ideal. A west-facing narrow site, a fixed road position, a neighbour's wall: compromises are sometimes needed. We lay out the options clearly, explain what each one means for light, ventilation and usable space, and let you decide with full information.

The pooja room deserves design

The pooja room is often the most personal space in a home. We treat it as a design project of its own: the right orientation, natural light where possible, carefully chosen materials, and lighting that makes it glow in the evening. In the Vanagaram residence, a backlit carved panel and brass details give the pooja space a quiet presence within a contemporary interior.
