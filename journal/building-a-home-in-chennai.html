Building Your Home in Chennai: From Plot to Possession

By Ar. Bharath Munusamy15 September 20268 min read

Building an independent house is one of the largest decisions a family makes. It is also a process most people go through only once. This guide sets out the stages we take our clients through, so you know what to expect and when your decisions matter most.

1. Before you design: the plot and the brief

Collect your plot documents, the survey sketch and any existing approvals. Know your setbacks and permitted floor area, because they set the size of what you can build. Then write down your brief: number of bedrooms, parking, elderly family members, work-from-home needs, and of course the budget. The more honest the brief, the better the design.

2. Concept design

We study the site, the sun and the street, and develop plan options. This is the stage to ask big questions: one floor or two, an internal courtyard or not, where the staircase goes. Changes here cost nothing but time. Changes after construction starts cost a great deal more.

3. Design development and 3D views

Once a plan is agreed, we develop elevations and photorealistic 3D views, so you can see the house as it will be. Materials, colours and window sizes are fixed here, along with a preliminary cost estimate.

4. Drawings and approvals

A buildable house needs a complete drawing set: architectural working drawings, structural drawings, and electrical and plumbing layouts. We also prepare the drawings needed for planning permission. Which authority you apply to depends on where your plot is, so we confirm this at the start.

A note on timingApprovals can take time. Start the paperwork early and avoid fixing a construction start date until the permission is in hand.

5. Construction

A typical sequence runs: foundation, plinth, columns and slabs floor by floor, brickwork, plastering, services, flooring, joinery, painting and finishing. A G+1 or G+2 home usually takes 10 to 14 months, depending on size, finishes and weather. Monsoon months slow outdoor work, so plan around them where possible.

The biggest risk during construction is a gap between drawings and site. That is why Inara takes responsibility for both design and execution: our civil team builds, and our designers check the work at every stage.

6. Interiors and handover

Interiors work best when they are planned with the architecture, not after it. Electrical points, ceiling heights and plumbing positions all depend on the interior layout. At handover we walk the house with you, close a snag list, and hand over drawings and warranties.

Five decisions worth getting right

  1. Orientation of living spaces and bedrooms.
  2. Staircase position, which shapes every floor.
  3. Window sizes and shading on west faces.
  4. Waterproofing of terraces and wet areas.
  5. Enough electrical points, in the right places.
